Staying Alive

The five survival rings, what drains each one, and what happens when one runs out.

On this page
  • The rings
  • What drains each bar
  • When a bar runs out
  • Getting health back
  • Your first hour

You spawn at 50% food and 50% water, and both start falling straight away. Water goes first: with nothing else going on it runs out in about 1 hour 40 minutes, so finding a drink is your first job.

The rings

Five rings sit on the right of your screen. Each shows its value, and a + or - appears beside it when that value is moving quickly, such as while a meal is still going in.

Ring What it shows Warning colors
Health How hurt you are Orange at 45% or less, red at 20% or less
Food How fed you are Orange at 45% or less, red at 20% or less
Water How hydrated you are Orange at 45% or less, red at 20% or less
Temperature Your body temperature in °C. 37 is normal. Blue at 35 °C or below, red at 39 °C or above
Blood How much blood you have left Orange at 45% or less, red at 20% or less

When you’ve stored warmth by a fire, the temperature ring shows ++ or +++ instead of a trend. Body Temperature explains how to build that store.

Your inventory screen shows the same stats, plus Sickness, which reads HEALTHY while you’re well. In Settings, under Interface, you can move the rings to the bottom right, hide them, or turn the inventory stats off.

Status icons

Four icons appear only when they apply:

  • Bleeding pulses while you’re losing blood.
  • Broken bone shows when a limb is hurt badly enough to slow you down or throw off your aim.
  • Sickness shows once you’re 5% sick, well before it starts costing you health.
  • Antibiotic shows while a dose is working, and pulses in its last 30 seconds.

What drains each bar

  • Food drops 0.7% every 2 minutes, so a full bar lasts about 4 hours 45 minutes. Sprinting burns about 0.7% more every minute.
  • Water drops 1% every 2 minutes, so a full bar lasts 3 hours 20 minutes. Sprinting burns 1.2% more every minute, which can empty a full bar in about an hour of running.
  • Health drops from wounds, from an empty food or water bar, and from serious sickness.
  • Blood drops while you bleed. See Wounds and Bleeding.
  • Temperature drops in cold air, rain, wind, water, at night and in wet clothes. See Body Temperature.
  • Food poisoning makes you vomit, and each time you lose food and water. See Cooking and Spoilage.

Your standing changes the clock. On the Hero path, food and water drain up to 20% slower. As a Bandit, they drain up to 30% faster.

When a bar runs out

Food or water at zero

Your character warns you on the way down, with a sound and a line on screen at 20%, 15%, 10%, 5% and 0%:

  • I’m feeling hungry… then I need to eat soon…, I need to eat now…, I’m starving… and I’m dying of starvation…
  • I’m feeling thirsty… then I need to drink something soon…, I need to drink something now…, I’m dehydrated… and I’m dying of dehydration…

At zero you lose health every 2 seconds, and a distress sound repeats about once a minute. It stops the moment the empty bar climbs above zero, so any food or drink buys you time. Hero perks cut this damage by up to 30%.

Temperature too low

Cold never takes health directly. It works through sickness:

  1. Below 32 °C, an exposure clock starts. After 25 minutes you begin to get sick. If your clothes are also wet, the clock runs twice as fast.
  2. Sickness builds slowly. At 50% sickness, your coughs start costing health and stamina.
  3. Standing under a roof, sitting in a closed vehicle or being in a safe zone stops sickness from building.

Sickness and Medicine covers the cure. Body Temperature covers how to warm up.

Health at zero

You die. How You Die lists the usual causes.

Getting health back

Food and water heal you, but only slowly and only on three conditions:

  • Food and water are both at 80% or more.
  • You haven’t taken damage for 5 minutes.
  • You’re conscious.

Then you recover about 3% of your health an hour, rising to about 6% an hour when both bars are full. Anything faster takes medical supplies: see Wounds and Bleeding.

Your first hour

  1. Drink. Water bottles, canteens, soda cans and well pumps all work. See Food and Water.
  2. Find food you can open. Canned food needs a tin opener or a bayonet.
  3. Stay dry. Wet clothes stop keeping you warm.
  4. Cook meat and fish before you eat them.
  5. Watch the icons. A bleeding icon matters more than any ring.
